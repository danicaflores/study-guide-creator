# ECON 2035 — Unit 3 notes: Demand, Supply, and Elasticity
(Sample practice material — not from a real syllabus)

Exam 1 objectives:
1. Distinguish a change in quantity demanded from a change in demand.
2. Identify shifters of demand and supply.
3. Find market equilibrium from demand and supply equations.
4. Compute and interpret price elasticity of demand (midpoint method).
5. Connect elasticity to total revenue.

Notes:
- Law of demand: as price rises, quantity demanded falls (ceteris paribus).
- Movement ALONG the curve = change in quantity demanded (caused by own price only).
- SHIFT of the curve = change in demand. Demand shifters: income, prices of related goods
  (substitutes, complements), tastes, expectations, number of buyers.
- Supply shifters: input prices, technology, expectations, number of sellers.
- Equilibrium: Qd = Qs. Class example: Qd = 100 - 2P, Qs = 20 + 2P → P* = 20, Q* = 60.
- Midpoint elasticity: Ed = [(Q2 - Q1) / ((Q2 + Q1)/2)] ÷ [(P2 - P1) / ((P2 + P1)/2)]
  Report as absolute value. |Ed| > 1 elastic, < 1 inelastic, = 1 unit elastic.
- Total revenue TR = P × Q. If demand is elastic, raising price lowers TR. If inelastic, raising price raises TR.
- Prof: "Half the class mixed up 'demand' vs 'quantity demanded' on the quiz. Know the difference!"
- In-class example: price of coffee rises from $4 to $6, quantity falls from 120 to 80 cups.
